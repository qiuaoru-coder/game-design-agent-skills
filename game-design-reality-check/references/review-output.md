# Reality-check report guide

Use this structure for a formal review. Omit sections that do not help the requested decision.

## 1. Executive reality check

State:

- verdict: Unexamined, Plausible but unproven, Test-ready, Provisionally supported, or Contradicted;
- the single weakest or most consequential link;
- current confidence and its main limitation;
- the immediate decision recommendation.

Do not declare a design “fun” or “not fun” from a proposal document.

## 2. Design promise and decision

Summarize the player, situation, repeated behavior, intended experience, design/product result, and decision the review should inform. Mark inferred material.

## 3. Claim and evidence ledger

| Claim | Type | Current evidence | Evidence label | Confidence limit | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ... | Player/Experience/Design/Production/Market | ... | Observed/Sourced/Derived/Assumed/Unknown | ... | ... |

Keep major claims separate. Do not use a confident overall score to hide one unsupported critical assumption.

## 4. Causal map

Use GMT internally and show it when useful:

| Goal | Means | Tools | Expected behavior | Claimed outcome | Link strength and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Flag missing links, orphan tools, contradictions, dominant strategies, execution dependencies, and player knowledge the design assumes.

## 5. Failure-first findings

Order findings by severity and decision value:

- **Critical**: core claim cannot currently follow or decisive evidence contradicts it.
- **Major**: high-impact assumption, missing support, alternative explanation, or feasibility mismatch.
- **Minor**: local clarity, balance, feedback, accessibility, or evidence weakness.
- **Opportunity**: optional improvement that does not block validation.

For each finding include observation, why it matters, evidence label, consequence, and recommended decision.

## 6. Assumptions and unknowns

Rank assumptions using impact, uncertainty, and cost of being wrong. Ask only questions that can change the causal map, priority, prototype, or decision.

## 7. Cheapest decisive test

Specify:

- central hypothesis and attached decision;
- target participant and play context;
- smallest playable scope;
- included and excluded mechanics/content;
- behaviors to observe before asking opinions;
- measurements or interview prompts;
- support, revise, and stop signals;
- confounds and next action for each possible result.

## 8. Action plan

Separate:

1. decide or clarify now;
2. prototype before further production;
3. revise or remove if contradicted;
4. build only after validation;
5. optional later opportunities.

## Optional scoring

Use scoring only when the user needs comparison or tracking. A 1–5 score may summarize clarity, causal coherence, evidence strength, test readiness, and feasibility. Always show the critical unsupported claim separately; an average cannot turn an unverified core promise into a supported one.

## Tone

Be candid without being theatrical. Challenge claims rather than the designer. Distinguish “the design is incoherent,” “the document does not explain it,” and “the claim is coherent but untested.” Preserve uncertainty instead of manufacturing a verdict.
